## The files offered and taken

The first project (Severson et al., the site's project of 2019-01-23) offers one MATLAB struct per batch, and low-rate data for the paper's Figure 4 and a structured-data archive, which were not taken. Its three batches are 2017-05-12, 2017-06-30 and 2018-04-12; each struct was taken. The second project (Attia et al., 2019-09-17) offers five batches: four closed-loop rounds (oed_0 to oed_3, started 2018-08-02, 2018-09-02, 2018-09-06 and 2018-09-10, structs of 320 to 340 MB) and the validation batch of 2019-01-24 ("batch9"). Only the validation batch was taken: the site says the cells of the four rounds were cycled 100 to 120 times and that temperature was logged only for the validation batch, and the paper says each round's experiments stopped after 100 cycles (p. 398), so the rounds hold neither a measured lifetime nor a temperature.

## The cell, the cycler, the chamber and the cycling

- The cell. Both project pages name the LFP/graphite A123 Systems APR18650M1A, 1.1 Ah nominal and 3.3 V. Severson et al. say the same (p. 384 and Methods, p. 389), and so do Attia et al. (Methods, Experimental), who add the packing date 2015-09-26 and the lot EL1508007-R. It is the LFP cell of the Sandia study of Stage 3.
- Cycler and chamber. A 48-channel Arbin LBT cycler with four-point cylindrical fixtures, in an Amerex Instruments environmental chamber at 30 °C (Severson et al., Methods, p. 389; Attia et al., Methods, Experimental). The project pages describe the chamber as forced convection, "set to 30°C", with the cells in horizontal fixtures.
- Charging. Severson et al.: 72 one-step and two-step policies from 0 to 80 % state of charge, written C1(Q1)-C2, charging in 9 to 13.3 minutes, then 1C CC-CV to 3.6 V (Methods, p. 389); the site gives about 8 to 13.3 minutes for the 2017-05-12 batch and 10 minutes for the other two. Attia et al.: four-step, ten-minute protocols written CC1-CC2-CC3-CC4, each step over 20 % of the state of charge, currents from 3.6C to 8C, then 1C CC-CV (Methods).
- Discharge. Every cell discharged at 4C, CC-CV to 2.0 V (Severson et al., p. 384 and Methods, current cut-off C/50; the site gives C/20 for the 2018-04-12 batch; Attia et al., Methods, cut-off C/20). Confirmed: these cells charge at several C and discharge at 4C, where Sandia charged at 0.5C and discharged at 1C or 2C (and other rates) in 15, 25 and 35 °C air.
- Internal resistance: the average of ten pulses of plus and minus 3.6C, 30 ms wide in 2017-05-12 and 2017-06-30 and 33 ms in 2018-04-12 and the validation batch, during charging at 80 % state of charge (both project pages; Severson et al., Methods).

## The cycle life

- Severson et al. define it as "the number of cycles until 80% of nominal capacity" (p. 384); the batch pages give 0.88 Ah (the 2017-06-30 page calls 0.88 Ah 75 % of nominal, which it is not: 0.88 Ah is 80 % of 1.1 Ah). Attia et al. define it as the number of cycles until the discharge capacity falls below 80 % of nominal (Methods), and their early predictor's target as cycles "to reach 80% of nominal capacity, or 0.88 A h" (Methods).
- The authors' code. `cell_analysis.m`, which built the structs, numbers a cell's summary entries 1 to k, one per completed cycle after the first logged one, and stores as `cycle_life` the first cycle whose discharge capacity is below 0.88 Ah and is followed by a lower one; the Python loader takes that stored field. `LoadData.m` labels each cell with the first cycle below 0.88 Ah, or one more than its number of cycles. The window "cycles 2 to 100" of the first paper's temperature and resistance features (Table 1 of the manuscript: maximum, minimum and integrated temperature over cycles 2 to 100; resistance at cycle 2, its minimum over cycles 2 to 100 and its difference between cycles 100 and 2) is summary entries 2 to 100, as `build_battery_features.m` indexes them; Severson et al. say their features use only data from the first 100 cycles (p. 386).

## The batches and their notes

- 2017-05-12 (the site's batch page). One-step and two-step policies, generally two cells per policy except 3.6C(80%); rests of 1 minute after charging to 80 % and 1 second after discharging; an initial C/10 cycle; constant-voltage cut-offs C/50. Notes: the computer restarted twice, leaving gaps; chamber control was "somewhat inconsistent", so the baseline chamber temperature varied; channels 4 and 8 never started; the thermocouples of channels 15 and 16 were swapped; cycle 1 is not in the struct; channels 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 were stopped at the end of the batch and resumed in the 2017-06-30 batch, with a rise in capacity on resuming; channels 13, 19, 21, 22 and 31 were stopped before reaching 80 %. Severson et al. give the same rests (Methods, p. 389).
- 2017-06-30. Ten-minute policies, generally one cell per policy, three for 4.8C(80%); five-minute rests after charging to 80 % and after discharging; an initial C/10 cycle; cut-offs C/50. Notes: a computer restart near cycle 250 for most policies gave an eight-hour rest; one cell (channel 3) was affected by a second restart near its end of life; the thermocouples of channels 7 and 21 were found detached from their cells at unloading; those of channels 15 and 16 were swapped; channels 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 continue the 2017-05-12 cells, "NOT new experiments" (the site's words); channel 10 died quickly and may be defective.
- 2018-04-12. Two-step ten-minute policies, three to eight cells per policy; four 5-second rests; a final C/10 cycle at 80 %; cut-offs C/20. Notes: resistance tests caused open-circuit errors and brief pauses; channels 33 and 41 were stopped before reaching 80 %; channel 46 had noisy voltage, probably a connection error.
- 2019-01-24, the validation batch. Nine protocols with five cells each, cycled to failure or beyond (the batch page; Attia et al., p. 400, who cycle "five batteries each to failure"). Notes: 45 cells started in every channel except 4, 5 and 48; an early schedule error and inconsistencies in the first cycle led to its removal; the cell in channel 46 failed at the start and two cells were started a few days later in channels 5 and 48, of which channel 5 did not start, keeping five cells per protocol; channel 12 gave an anomalous early prediction but cycled to failure; computer issues paused channels 45 and 48 twice before their end of life. The authors' validation README names channel 46 (bad contact) and channel 12 as the anomalous predictions.

## The can temperature

The project pages and Severson et al. (Methods, p. 389) describe a type T thermocouple fixed with thermal epoxy (OMEGATHERM 201) and Kapton tape to the bare can after a strip of the insulation was removed. On its reliability both project pages say the measurements are "not perfectly reliable" and that the thermocouple "sometimes loses contact during cycling", the contact varying substantially from cell to cell. Severson et al. leave four cells out of their full model because some probes lost contact (Figure 3's caption, p. 385), and trace jumps in their prediction error to temperature fluctuations of the chamber (Figure 5's caption, p. 388).

## Exclusions and carried cells

- The Python loader (`Load Data.ipynb`): from 2017-05-12 it removes the cells at struct indices 8, 10, 12, 13 and 22 as batteries that do not reach 80 %; it appends the 2017-06-30 cells at indices 7, 8, 9, 15 and 16 to the 2017-05-12 cells at indices 0 to 4, adding to their cycle lives the numbers of cycles of those continuations, which the loader writes as 662, 981, 1060, 208 and 482 (seen when the code was read), and removes them from 2017-06-30; from 2018-04-12 it removes indices 37, 2, 23, 32, 42 and 43 as noisy channels. That leaves 41, 43 and 40 cells, the paper's 124, which it splits into a training set (41), a primary test set (43) and a secondary test set (40) (p. 386). Severson et al. add that four cells had unexpectedly high measurement noise and were excluded (Methods, p. 389).
- `LoadData.m` does the same with its own rule for 2018-04-12: it removes the cell of channel 46 for a data-collection problem, then the cells whose last discharge capacity is above 0.885 Ah, then three noisy cells.
- The second paper: `Validation/electrochem/save_validation_electrochem_data.m` sorts the 2019-01-24 struct by policy and loops over 45 cells, taking each cell's stored `cycle_life`.

## Temperature and heat as a cause

- Severson et al. note that while the chamber is controlled the cells' temperatures vary by up to 10 °C within a cycle, from the heat of charge and discharge, as a function of internal impedance and policy (pp. 384 and 385), and list temperature features among their candidates; they do not read heat as a cause of the differences in lifetime.
- Attia et al. find the best protocols close to single-step constant current, where the literature's protocols step down to avoid lithium plating, and write: "We speculate that minimizing parasitic reactions caused by heat generation may be the operative optimization strategy" (p. 399), pointing to their Supplementary Discussion 3, which is not in the PDF read. They also tuned their algorithm on a simulator in which degradation grows with temperature through an Arrhenius law, stating that it is not designed to represent degradation accurately (Methods, physics-based simulator). The second paper therefore does read heat as a possible reason, and Part C asks the authors' own question.

## The 2025 dataset (Part A0.9, a survey)

van Vlijmen et al., Aging matrix visualizes complexity of battery aging across hundreds of cycling protocols, Energy and Environmental Science 18, 6641 to 6654 (2025), https://doi.org/10.1039/d4ee05609d; data at https://data.matr.io/11/ under CC BY-NC 4.0 ("This dataset is released under CC BY-NC 4.0"). Only the metadata archive was read (`metadata.zip`, 51,659 bytes, SHA-256 eb28c535ea1a899191e8e739dab70ba5ece10a80772c9754a773464fb5740ef4), with the article and its supplementary information as RSC serves them; nothing from it is redistributed here.

- The cell: 21700 cylindrical NCA/graphite + SiOx cells, Panasonic, harvested from a 2019 Tesla Model 3, 4.84 Ah at low rate (the article, section 2.1; its supplementary information, section S.2.1). The Geslin cells are also commercial SiOx-graphite/NCA EV energy cells, but Geslin et al. name neither the maker nor the model in the main text, so whether they are the same cell is not established.
- Chamber temperature: every cell cycled in a chamber set to 25 °C, and the temperature was not varied (the article, Figures 1 and 2 and section 2.1; supplementary Tables S2, S3 and S5). No protocol ran at more than one chamber temperature.
- Replicates: yes. The metadata's exclusion table lists protocols with two to eight cells; the article counts 359 cells under 207 protocols.
- Cell temperature: neither the article nor its supplementary information mentions a thermocouple or a logged cell temperature, and the metadata holds no temperature field.
- So the dataset does not hold the Geslin cell's lifetime against temperature near 35 C: it has one chamber temperature, 25 °C, and no reported cell temperature. Stage 6 continues.
