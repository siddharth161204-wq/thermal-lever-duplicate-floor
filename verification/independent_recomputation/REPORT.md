## 1. Verdict in brief

- **Law 1 (Kucinskis et al. 2022)**: lever, the three floors in both forms (f/s and 2 atanh(f/2)/s), the per-rate secants at 0.2C, 0.4C, 0.6C and the lifetime curve agree to round-off (largest relative difference 6e-14): s(35 C) = -0.0098910 per K, Delta T_floor(8.7 %) = -8.8137 K. The assumed-noise Monte Carlo agrees within its sampling noise. **D16's hand figure for the secant's standard error (0.0013 per K) is too large by sqrt(2)**: the registered C/2 secant averages two printed values at each temperature, so its standard error is 0.0187/20 = 0.00094 per K, which is what the published Monte Carlo itself shows. Results unaffected.
- **Law 2 (Schimpe et al. 2018)**: L(35 C), s(35 C), every Delta T_floor, the term levers and the text-value levers agree to 3e-9 or better at all six rates: s(35 C, C/2) = 0.051877 per K, Delta T_floor(8.7 %) = 1.6804 K. **V1 at 25 C disagrees by 3.4 %**: the published 8.60 % is the sum of the three square-root terms only; Eq. 23's fourth (low-temperature high-SOC) term, which the lever does include, adds 0.31 points at 25 C and 1C, giving 8.91 %. V1 still passes ("less than 9 %"), with a 0.09-point margin instead of 0.40. The equation forms could not be checked against the paper (Section 6).
- **Lumped module (2x4 in air) and cold plate (1x8)**: all 36 cases agree to 4e-10 or better in Delta T_module, T_max, T_min, T_mean, hottest minus mean, hottest and coldest positions and energy balance; the two-node reduction reproduces the eight-node spread exactly; every published sensitivity row is reproduced exactly.

Within the 3 % tolerance, explained by the published tetrahedral mesh's discretisation (Section 4, item 3). At h = 10: C/2 0.0920 K published, 0.0931 K converged; 2C 1.472 K published, 1.489 K converged.
- **Implied divergence, Step 3, predictions**: every exceedance flag, every Step 3 lowest rate and every prediction score is reproduced (P1 failed, P2 held, P3 held, P4 failed, P5 failed). Cell-derived implied divergences inherit the 1.2 % cell difference. D19's cycle-averaged heat and module spreads agree to 2e-10.

## 4. Discrepancies beyond 1 % (beyond 3 % for the resolved cell), with diagnosis

1. **V1, Law 2, 25 C: published 8.6021 %, mine 8.9090 % (-3.4 %).** The published value equals (to 1e-9) the sum of the calendar, high-temperature and low-temperature square-root terms (3.0345 + 1.8924 + 3.6752 %). Eq. 23 as registered in D4 also has the low-temperature high-SOC term k_HS Q_Ch above SOC_Ref. At 25 C (T = T_Ref) and a 1C charge (I_Ch = I_Ch,Ref = 3 A) both its factors are 1, so it adds 2.03e-6 per Ah x 0.18 x 8400 Ah = 0.307 points. The lever does include this term (the published lifetime curve at 15 C, 6085 cycles, matches mine with it, not without it, 6529), so only the V1 evaluation drops it. At 45 C the term is 0.0008 points (published 10.3527 %, mine 10.3535 %). V1 still passes at both temperatures (8.91 % is below "less than 9 %" and inside the 0 to 11 % acceptance), but the 25 C margin is 0.09 points, not 0.40, and V1 as published did not test the full model it was meant to test.
2. **D16 hand figure: 0.0013 per K stated, 0.00094 per K by propagation (+39 %).** sqrt(2) x 0.0187/20 is the error of one printed rate's secant (one value at 25 C, one at 45 C). The registered C/2 lever averages ln L over 0.4C and 0.6C at each temperature, so four independent values enter with weight 1/2: SE = 0.0187/20 = 0.00094 per K. The published Monte Carlo agrees with this, not with D16: its 2.5 to 97.5 % range [-0.01172, -0.00808] corresponds to a standard deviation of 0.00093 per K (mine 0.000937). Only the text figure is affected.
3. **Largest within-tolerance difference: the resolved cell.** Hottest minus coldest 1.20 % and hottest minus volume mean 2.25 % below my converged values in every air-cooled case (the spreads are linear in the heat, so the offset is a property of the mesh), 0.14 % and 0.45 % in the base-cooled case. Diagnosis: discretisation of the published refine-1 tetrahedral mesh, whose radial temperature differences are too small by about 1.5 %. Evidence from the repository's own outputs: (a) its V3 (e) analytic radial check gives 0.100541 K against the analytic 0.102129 K (-1.55 %); (b) its refine-2 values are 0.83 % above refine 1, and a second-order Richardson estimate from its two meshes (0.093087, 0.093012, 0.092789 K at h = 5, 10, 25) is within 0.10 % of my converged values (0.093182, 0.093108, 0.092886 K); (c) the base-cooled spread, mostly axial along the well-resolved k_z = 25 W/mK direction, differs by only 0.14 %; (d) the loss-carrying volume-mean and surface-mean rises agree to 3e-4 or better. Derived quantities inherit the 1.2 %: implied cell divergences, "max_implied_cell", the P3 values. No conclusion changes: P3 holds by a factor of five, and the closest cell-based Step 3 comparison (Law 2, 8.7 % floor, 2C) sits 11 % below its threshold in my values and 12 % in the published ones.

No other quantity differs by more than 1 % (0.33 % at most, the Monte Carlo percentiles).

## 5. DECISIONS.md: ambiguities, and places where the results do not follow it

1. **D4/D15, V1**: evaluates three of Eq. 23's four terms although D4 and D15 call for the full model (Section 4, item 1).
2. **D16**: the hand standard error is that of a single printed rate, not of the registered C/2 secant (Section 4, item 2).
3. **D18 range restriction not applied to Law 2 at 2C.** D18 reports the exact pair difference and exact worst-of-8 shortfall "where the cells lie inside the law's temperature range". implied_divergence.csv leaves them blank for Law 1 at 2C, as it should, but reports them for Law 2 at 2C (0.288 at h = 10) where every module cell is at 55.2 to 86.7 C, outside Law 2's fitted 0 to 55 C. The values agree with mine (Eq. 23 extrapolated) but should be blank or flagged.
4. **D4, "time average" of the calendar factor.** Whether the mean of k_Cal over the cycle (as used) or its root mean square is consistent depends on the form of the paper's rate-based Eq. 24, which I could not read (dQ/dt = k/(2 sqrt(t)) gives the mean; a virtual-time form gives the RMS). With the RMS the SOC factor is 1.2145 instead of 1.0989 and L(35 C, C/2) falls from 3458 to 3045 cycles, but s changes only from 0.051877 to 0.051898 per K (+0.04 %). The lever, floors and scores are insensitive.
5. **D3/D4, "centred difference of 0.5 K"**: the published Law 2 values follow +-0.5 K (a 1 K stencil); +-0.25 K and the exact derivative differ by 7e-5 and 9e-5 relative. No effect for Law 1.
6. **D17, the "exact pair" column for the worst-of-8 floor**: lever.json applies 2 atanh(f/2)/s to all three floors. For the worst-of-8 shortfall, a (mean - min)/mean quantity, this is not an exact inversion: in the symmetric 2x4 module (four cells at each of two temperatures) the shortfall is tanh(s Delta T/2), so the hottest-minus-coldest spread producing it is 2 atanh(f)/s, about twice f/s. D17 already names hottest minus mean (exactly half of hottest minus coldest in this module) as the matching spread and the registered comparison follows the brief's literal form, so this is a labelling point.
7. **comparison.json "share_of_loss_at_35C"**: normalised over the three square-root terms, not the loss (1e-5 to 5e-5 relative).
8. **D10's account of the received radiation form** mentions the quarter area but not that the received script used eps rather than eps^2 for the pair exchange; "radiation_as_received" follows the received script in both respects and is reproduced exactly (0.654548 K; with eps^2 it would be 0.657696 K).
9. **D6, "about 5 % larger with 1.24 W/mK"**: my solver gives +3.9 % at C/2, h = 10 (0.09678 against 0.09311 K); the 5 % is the pure-radial ratio 1.3/1.24, diluted by the axial path and the shell. A hand estimate; nothing depends on it.
10. **D15 V3 (c) hand offsets** (2.4, 3.6, 7.1 % of the rise) are infinite-cylinder estimates; computed offsets are 1.78, 2.64, 5.20 % (mine) and 1.77, 2.62, 5.17 % (published), so (c) fails as D15 said it would, by less than the hand estimate.
