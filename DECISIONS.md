# Decisions

Every choice that the brief does not fix is recorded here in full sentences, with its reason, before the runs it affects. D1 to D20 were written before the predictions were committed, D21 before the push, and D22 to D24 after the results; anything added later is marked as such.

## Inputs

D1. The floor is copied, not recomputed, from `results/results.json` of the published re-analysis (Satte 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23205413; github.com/siddharth161204-wq/geslin-floor-pilot at tag v1.0.1, commit 103f7e7; file SHA-256 be63577f...b8ac, full hash in `inputs/floor.json`). The exact values are used, not the rounded ones in the brief's script: mean 2.991 %, worst-of-8 shortfall 4.097 %, 95th percentile 8.718 % of the duplicate-pair lifetime difference at 90 % state of health. The names keep the rounded labels (3.0 %, 4.1 %, 8.7 %).

D2. The brief named Wang et al. 2011 (J. Power Sources 196, 3942) and Waldmann et al. 2014 (J. Power Sources 262, 129, doi:10.1016/j.jpowsour.2014.03.112, checked against the Crossref record) as examples. Neither full text could be read in this study: both are subscription articles, and ScienceDirect answered with a CAPTCHA, which was not bypassed. A Crossref query for Wang et al. 2011 was refused by a rate limit. A law whose table cannot be read cannot be copied, so neither is used. Two open-access laws whose full texts were read are used instead. They were chosen by a rule fixed before any of their numbers were used for a lever: (i) an explicit temperature dependence of cycle ageing; (ii) full text in hand, with the parameters printed in a table or as printed values; (iii) the chemistry closest to the dataset's SiOx-graphite/NCA 21700 cells first, then an Arrhenius-type law of another chemistry as the brief's bound.

D3. Law 1 is Kucinskis, Bozorgchenani, Feinauer, Kasper, Wohlfahrt-Mehrens and Waldmann, "Arrhenius plots for Li-ion battery ageing as a function of temperature, C-rate, and ageing state: An experimental study", J. Power Sources 549 (2022) 232129, doi:10.1016/j.jpowsour.2022.232129, CC BY 4.0 (read in full from the open repository copy at dspace.lu.lv). The cells are commercial 5.0 Ah 21700 cells with an NCA (LiNi0.90Co0.05Al0.05O2) plus about 6 % LiNiO2 cathode and a graphite anode with about 3 % Si (Table 1, p. 2; Section 2.1, pp. 2 to 3), the closest published match to the dataset's cells found. They were charged CC-CV at 0.2C, 0.4C or 0.6C to 4.2 V with CV to 0.05C, discharged at 0.5C CC to 2.5 V, in climate chambers at ambient temperatures up to 55 C (Fig. 7 tabulates -10 to 55 C), at least two cells per condition except one at 55 C (p. 3). The paper gives no activation-energy table; its fits are drawn in Fig. 3 only. It does print, in Fig. 7 (p. 8), the number of full cycles each condition took to reach 95, 90, 85 and 80 % SoH. Those printed values are the law: the lifetime to 90 % SoH is Fig. 7b. They are transcribed in `inputs/kucinskis2022_fig7_cycles.csv`. ln L is interpolated linearly in temperature between the printed temperatures from 15 to 55 C, and linearly in charge rate between the printed rates. The dataset charges every cell at C/2 (Geslin et al. 2025, Methods), so the registered lever of Law 1 is at a C/2 charge, the midpoint of 0.4C and 0.6C in ln L; the three printed rates are reported as a secondary result. The sensitivity at 35 C by the script's centred difference of 0.5 K equals the centred secant (ln L(45 C) - ln L(25 C)) / 20 K, which is also the slope at 35 C of the parabola through the three printed points; the printed grid allows no finer resolution. The paper tested one discharge rate, 0.5C, so Law 1 carries no discharge-rate dependence; the same lever is used at every rate and this is a stated limit (slower discharges spend more time per cycle, which shifts weight toward the time-driven high-temperature branch). The paper reports no parameter uncertainty, so there is no Monte Carlo over stated errors (D16).

D4. Law 2 is Schimpe, von Kuepach, Naumann, Hesse, Smith and Jossen, "Comprehensive modeling of temperature-dependent degradation mechanisms in lithium iron phosphate batteries", J. Electrochem. Soc. 165 (2018) A181-A193, doi:10.1149/2.1181714jes, CC BY 4.0 (read in full from the publisher's open-access PDF). The cell is a Sony US26650FTC1 LiFePO4/graphite 26650, 3 Ah in the study (Table I, p. A182). The full model of Eq. 23 (p. A188) is used, not one of its terms: calendar loss k_Cal(T,SOC) sqrt(t), high-temperature cycle loss k_Cyc,HighT(T) sqrt(Q_Tot), low-temperature cycle loss k_Cyc,LowT(T,I_Ch) sqrt(Q_Ch) and low-temperature high-SOC loss k(T,I_Ch,SOC) Q_Ch, with the stress factors of Eqs. 9, 15, 18 and 21 and every parameter from Table IV (p. A190), the anode potential of Eqs. A1 and A2 with the stoichiometries of Table AI (p. A193), SOC_Ref = 82 % and C_0 = 3 Ah (pp. A187 to A188). The paper fitted the model from 0 to 55 C at CC and CC-CV rates of 0.25C, 0.5C and 1C. Including the calendar term is not a conversion of a calendar dependence into a cycle one: it is the paper's own superposition for a cycling cell, which spends time at temperature in every cycle, and the decomposition by term is reported. The dataset-style protocol is mapped as follows, fixed here: CC charge at C/2 (I_Ch = 1.5 A for the 3 Ah cell) from 0 to 100 % SOC, CC discharge at the average rate back to 0 %, no rests; per full cycle 2 C_0 of total throughput, C_0 of charge throughput, (1 - SOC_Ref) C_0 of it above SOC_Ref, and 1/c_ch + 1/c_dis hours; the calendar factor is its time average over the cycle, in which the SOC is uniform from 0 to 1 in both ramps. The loss is the fraction of the original capacity; the lifetime is the number of full cycles (equal to equivalent full cycles in the dataset's definition) to a loss of 0.10. The table values are registered; the more precise values printed in the text (for example 32699 against 3.27e4 J/mol) are a secondary check.

D5. The brief's reasoning without data takes s as Ea/(R T^2). That holds for a loss linear in throughput. For a loss proportional to throughput to the power z, the lifetime at a fixed loss scales as k^(-1/z), so s = Ea/(z R T^2); Law 2's square-root terms give twice Ea/(R T^2). This changes no definition, only the hand estimates in predictions.md.

## Proxy cell and heat

D6. The proxy cell is the LG M50 of the two-cell study (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23253738): diameter 21.1 mm, length 70.1 mm, jelly-roll conductivity 1.3 W/mK through the layers and 25 W/mK along them (from O'Regan et al. 2022 through PyBaMM's ORegan2022 set, as computed in that study's `inputs/oregan2022_properties.txt`, copied here unchanged, SHA-256 in `provenance/SHA256SUMS`), a 0.25 mm steel can at 15 W/mK. At steady state only the conductivities and areas matter; density and specific heat do not enter. The ORegan2022 set gives 1.24 W/mK through the layers at 35 C; the two-cell model's constant 1.3 W/mK is kept so the resolved cell is that study's cell unchanged, and the within-cell spread would be about 5 % larger with 1.24 W/mK. The dataset's cells are a different 21700 (SiOx-graphite/NCA); the proxy sets magnitudes, not the dataset's exact values.

D7. The capacity used to turn a C-rate into a current is 5.0 Ah, the LG M50's rated capacity between 2.5 and 4.2 V (Chen et al. 2020, J. Electrochem. Soc. 167, 080534, p. 9).

D8. The resistance has to be derived. The brief named O'Regan et al. 2022 (Electrochim. Acta 425, 140700) or Chen et al. 2020 for a published DC resistance of the same cell. Chen et al. 2020 was read in full and reports no full-cell DC resistance (its Table VI gives half-cell charge-transfer resistances only). The full text of O'Regan et al. 2022 could not be read (ScienceDirect CAPTCHA; the Warwick repository record holds no file; the ChemRxiv abstract mentions no resistance measurement). The resistance is therefore computed with PyBaMM 26.9.0.0, Doyle-Fuller-Newman model, ORegan2022 parameter set (which carries the temperature dependence O'Regan et al. measured), isothermal at 35 C, on the rated 2.5 to 4.2 V window (`src/lgm50_resistance.py`, output `inputs/lgm50_resistance.json`). The registered value is the resistance whose I^2 R reproduces the model's own time-averaged irreversible heat (total minus reversible) over a full C/2 discharge and a full C/2 charge, averaged: 18.71 mOhm. The same quantity is 18.1 to 20.2 mOhm from C/10 to 2C, so one value serves every rate within 8 %. For comparison: a 10 s discharge pulse at C/2 from rest at 50 % SOC gives 15.47 mOhm and a 30 s pulse 20.37 mOhm. The model has no contact or tab resistance (the parameter set's value is 0 ohm), so a real cell's heat would be somewhat higher; the measured 1 kHz resistance of the NCA 21700 of Law 1 is 12.9 mOhm (Kucinskis et al. 2022, p. 3), the ohmic floor of a similar cell. The heat per cell is (c x 5 A)^2 x 18.71 mOhm: 1.8 mW at C/16, 4.7 mW at C/10, 18.7 mW at C/5, 117 mW at C/2, 0.468 W at 1C and 1.87 W at 2C. Every module and cell spread scales almost linearly with this heat, so the results can be rescaled for another resistance. The C/16 charge of the model stopped at 4.15 Ah before 4.2 V and its value (42.6 mOhm) is not used; the dataset charges at C/2.

## Module network

D9. Geometry and paths as the brief states: two rows of four at 23.1 mm pitch, a 2 mm air gap, one nickel strip per neighbour pair (horizontal and vertical neighbours, ten pairs), convection to 35 C air on every exposed can surface including both ends, and grey radiation at emissivity 0.8. The strip conductance per strip is the two-cell study's nickel value (its D24): 90 W/mK x 0.2 mm x 8 mm / 23.1 mm = 6.23 mW/K, two strips giving the brief's 12 mW/K. Air-gap conduction is k_air A_face / gap with k_air = 0.03 W/mK and A_face a quarter of the lateral area, as the two-cell study used: 17.4 mW/K per pair. Air at 35 C conducts about 0.027 W/mK; the two-cell study's 0.03 W/mK is kept for consistency.

D10. Radiation between neighbours follows the two-cell study's D5, which the author decided on 7 October 2026 for the identical received form: the view factor of parallel equal cylinders, F = 0.160 at a 2 mm gap, is defined per unit of the whole lateral area, so the script as received, which multiplied it by a quarter of the lateral area, understated the exchange by a factor of four. Each pair exchanges eps^2 sigma F A_lat (Ti^4 - Tj^4), and each cell radiates to the room over A_outer minus n eps F A_lat, where n is its number of neighbours (first order in (1 - eps) F, room as a black surface). Diagonal neighbours are not coupled, as in the brief's neighbour list.

D11. Convection follows the two-cell study's D6: no convection across a 2 mm gap, so each cell's convective area is its outer area minus a quarter of the lateral area per neighbour, which is exactly the received script's exposed area. The received script also used this exposed area for radiation to the room; D10 replaces that.

D12. The cold-plate arrangement is run as a secondary result, with no prediction: one row of eight at the same pitch, the bottom end of every cell on a plate at 35 C through 600 W/(m2 K) over the base area (the base conductance of the published cell study, doi:10.2139/ssrn.7570666), the rest of each cell in 35 C air with h = 5, 10 and 25 W/(m2 K) and radiation as in D10. The same base condition is applied to the resolved cell at h = 10, also as a secondary result.

## Resolved cell

D13. Step 2(b) reuses the two-cell study's scikit-fem cell: `src/twocell/resolved_cell2.py`, `mesh_cell2.py` and `lumped_two_cell.py` are copied unchanged from github.com/siddharth161204-wq/m50-two-cell-pilot at tag v1.0.0, commit 2b35ac7 (full SHA-256 in `provenance/SHA256SUMS`). The driver `src/resolved_cell_steady.py` changes nothing in them; it uses the mesh, the anisotropic conduction operator and the can shell, and (1) removes the neighbour: no strips, no gap conduction, no radiation from a neighbour, convection over the whole outer surface; (2) sets 35 C ambient, h = 5, 10, 25 W/(m2 K) and radiation at emissivity 0.8 from the whole outer surface; (3) spreads the uniform internal heat per unit volume of the cylinder; (4) solves the steady state directly by Newton iteration on the same operators instead of time stepping.

D14. Reported per case: Delta T_cell = hottest node minus coldest node, the hottest node minus the volume-average temperature, the area-average outer-surface temperature and where the hottest and coldest nodes are.

## Verification

D15. V1 and V2 as defined in `src/verify_pre.py` run before the predictions: V1 for Law 1 checks the transcription of the printed table against the values and statements in the paper's text; V1 for Law 2 checks the model against the capacity loss the paper states after 2800 full cycles at 1C at 25 C ("less than 9%") and 45 C ("12%"), within the paper's own 2 percentage point model error. V2 checks the energy balance of the module network on non-registered inputs and on registered inputs with the strip at 1e3 W/K, and the uniformity of the eight nodes at 1e3 W/K. V3 runs after the predictions are public, because any resolved run carries the within-cell spread per watt. V3 is fixed now: (a) the steady energy balance closes within 1e-8 of the source; (b) the area-average outer-surface temperature, which sets the losses, matches the lumped cell with the same losses within 1 % of the rise above ambient; (c) the brief's literal check, the volume-average temperature against the lumped cell within 1 % of the rise, is reported, and is expected to fail for a physical reason: a lumped cell has its surface at the mean temperature, while a cell with a uniform internal source is hotter inside than at its surface, by about q / (8 pi k_r L) between the volume average and the surface for radial conduction, which is 2.4, 3.6 and 7.1 % of the rise at h = 5, 10 and 25 (hand estimate); (d) one mesh refinement (every spacing halved, the two-cell study's refine 2) changes Delta T_cell by less than 5 %; (e) the roll operator alone reproduces the analytic steady radial solution with a lateral Robin condition within 1 %. Numerical acceptance is (a), (b), (d) and (e).

D16. Uncertainty. Neither law states parameter errors, so the brief's Monte Carlo over stated standard errors does not apply and the spread across the two laws is reported. Secondary, and labelled as an assumption: if each printed cycle count of Law 1 carried the dataset's per-cell lifetime noise (2.65 %, the floor study's sigma at 90 % SOH) averaged over two cells, the centred secant would carry a standard error of sqrt(2) x 0.0187 / 20 K = 0.0013 per kelvin; this is propagated by Monte Carlo (seed 20261010) as an indication only.

## Comparison and scoring

D17. Delta T_floor = f / s is the registered form (the brief's). The exact inversion for a pair, 2 atanh(f/2) / s, is reported alongside; it differs by less than 0.1 %. Where s is negative, hotter cells live longer and Delta T_floor is negative; comparisons with a temperature spread use its magnitude. The pair floors (3.0 % and 8.7 %) compare with the hottest-minus-coldest spread; the worst-of-8 shortfall compares, as the brief writes it, with the same spread, and the hottest-minus-mean spread, which is the matching quantity for a shortfall below the module mean, is reported alongside.

D18. The implied lifetime divergence is s x Delta T (registered). Secondary: the law evaluated at each cell's own temperature gives the exact pair difference between the hottest and coldest cells and the exact worst-of-8 shortfall, where the cells lie inside the law's temperature range.

D19. Secondary, with no prediction: the dataset's own protocol charges every cell at C/2, so the cycle-averaged heat of a dataset cell is not the heat at its average discharge rate. For CC discharge at rate c and CC charge at C/2, the cycle-averaged I^2 R heat is R I_1C^2 (0.5 + c) / (2 + 1/c), against R I_1C^2 c^2 for the brief's heat: 8.0 times larger at C/16, 5.0 at C/10, 2.5 at C/5 and equal at C/2. Dynamic discharge profiles raise it further by the mean square of the current over the square of its mean. The module spread and implied divergence are also reported at this cycle-averaged heat for the dataset's four rates.

## Added after the predictions commit, before the push and before any run

D21. The steady resolved solve of D13 uses Newton iteration on the temperature rise with each step solved by conjugate gradients preconditioned by smoothed-aggregation algebraic multigrid (pyamg 5.3.0), to a relative residual of 1e-13, instead of a direct sparse solve: the direct solve took 46 s per case on the base mesh (39,990 nodes, half model) and ran out of memory on the refined mesh of V3 (297,057 nodes). D24. Post-hoc analyses, not pre-registered, in `src/posthoc.py` (outputs `results/posthoc.json` and `results/dataset_protocol_heat.csv`), prompted by the independent checks; none changes a registered number. (1) The dataset's own discharge profiles. The brief's heat is that of a constant current at the average rate, and D19 named, without quantifying, the extra heat of a dynamic discharge, mean(I^2) / mean(I)^2. The dataset's authors publish each cell's mean discharge current c and its normalized current variance NCV (github.com/geslina/dynamic_cycling_Nature_Energy_2024 at commit 5b2f7f0, `data/protocol_features.csv`, with the names in `data/metadata.pkl` read through the floor study's whitelist; both files hash-checked). NCV is the variance over the mean, in C-rate units: for the constant-current protocols with a storage rest, two-level profiles, c + NCV equals the measured current of the same rate without storage within 3e-6, and for storage at full charge the rest fraction equals NCV / (c + NCV) within 3e-6; for constant current without rest NCV is zero. Per unit of discharged capacity the discharge window (rests included) lasts 1/c hours with mean(I^2) = c^2 + c NCV. The dataset's charge is CC-CV at C/2 to 4.2 V with a C/20 cut-off; its duration t_ch (2.23 h) and its integral of (I / I_1C)^2, e_ch (0.481 h), per unit of discharged capacity, come from PyBaMM's Doyle-Fuller-Newman model of the proxy cell (ORegan2022, isothermal 35 C, discharge to 3.1 V), the model behind the registered resistance. The cycle-averaged heat is R I_1C^2 (c + NCV + e_ch) / (1/c + t_ch); D19's 2 h constant-current charge (e_ch = 0.5, t_ch = 2) gives 3 to 8 % more and is kept alongside. The module is solved at that heat for each of the 92 cells; the resolved cell's spread is scaled from its C/2 value; the implied divergence uses the registered lever at 35 C, with Law 2 at each cell's own temperature (D18) alongside, and the worst-of-8 floor is also compared with the hottest-minus-mean spread (D17). The steady spread at the heat of the discharge window alone is reported too, since the cells approach it while that window lasts. The audit found that at C/2 Law 2 reaches the mean floor with 1.48 times the constant-current heat at h = 10 (1.58 at h = 5, 1.66 at h = 25). (2) The thermal time constant of a module cell (heat capacity from `inputs/oregan2022_properties.txt` and the two-cell study's steel can of about 9 g at 500 J/(kg K); loss conductance from the network), 0.19 to 0.46 h. (3) V1 for Law 2 with all four terms (D23). (4) The air conductivity of 0.03 W/(m K) and the can's 15 W/(m K) are inherited from the two-cell study, which gives no primary table for them, and the nickel's 90 W/(m K) gives the brief's strip value; the C/2 module spread with the air conductivity 10 % lower and higher is reported.

## Stage 2: the lever measured on the KIT LG INR18650HG2 ageing dataset

Stage 2 was first planned on the Sandia dataset of Preger et al. 2020 (the code on this branch's first commit). batteryarchive.org sends those files only on e-mail request, so Stage 2 uses the KIT dataset of Luh and Blank instead; the Sandia study will run unchanged as Stage 3, with its own predictions, if its files arrive. D25 to D35 were written, and `predictions_stage2.md` committed and pushed, before any capacity, temperature, date or other value of the KIT cell data was read. Stage 1's code and results are not edited.

D25. The data. Version 2, result data, of Luh and Blank's dataset (doi:10.35097/1969, CC BY 4.0), checked as `inputs/kit/MANIFEST.md` records. Read before the predictions: the archive's file list, sizes and hashes; readme.txt and "Readme v2.pdf"; the configuration files (cfg.zip); the "Data Structure v05.3.xlsx" definitions of the CFG and EOC columns and the state codes; the column names of the EOC files; the check-up schedule; the cell count per parameter set. Of the "Cycling Experiment Cell Overview" spreadsheet only the sheet names and first header row were read: the configuration files give every cell's parameter set, temperature, window and rates, so the spreadsheet was not needed. Not opened at all: the plausibility-check spreadsheet, the result plots, the EIS and pulse files, the log data, and the dataset authors' results publications. "Readme v2.pdf" describes the capacity fade qualitatively (section 3); that text was read, as the brief allows.

D26. The cells and protocols. The configuration files hold 240 cells: 60 calendar, 36 profile and 144 cyclic (age_type 2). The 144 cyclic cells form 48 parameter sets (P017 to P064) of three cells each, numbered 1 to 3 within the set (parameter_nr), at operating temperatures 0, 10, 25 and 40 C. A protocol is a window and a rate pair; there are twelve, each run at all four temperatures: windows 0-100 % (2.5 to 4.2 V), 10-100 % (3.249 to 4.2 V) and 10-90 % (3.249 to 4.092 V), and charge/discharge rates 0.33/0.33, 0.33/1.00, 1.00/1.00 and 1.67/1.00 C (1, 3 and 5 A charge; 1 and 3 A discharge). The table is `inputs/kit/cyclic_cells.csv`. The nominal capacity is 3.0 Ah (the readme and the data structure). Only the cyclic cells are used.

D27. Check-up capacity. The EOC files hold one row per run (a charge or a discharge). The check-up capacity is the 1/3 C (1 A) CC-CV discharge from 4.2 V to 2.5 V with a C/20 (0.15 A) cut-off at room temperature (the readme): within each check-up (EOC rows with cyc_condition 2, CHECKUP_RT, grouped by num_cycles_checkup), the discharging rows (cyc_charged 0) with v_min_target_V 2.5 V and i_dischg_min_A -1.0 A, and of those the one with the largest |delta_q_dischg_Ah|, whose |delta_q_dischg_Ah| is the capacity. The EFC of a check-up is the cell's total discharge in any mode before that run, (total_q_dischg_sum_Ah - |delta_q_dischg_Ah|) / 3.0 Ah. Every check-up of the cell counts, the manual last one (CU 29, air-cooled) included. Rows with timestamp_origin 5 (inserted, no cycler data) are not used anywhere.

D28. Lifetime and censoring. L is the EFC at which the check-up capacity first falls below 90 % of the cell's first check-up capacity, by linear interpolation between the two bracketing check-ups (definition 1); 80 % is the secondary threshold. A cell that never falls below is censored at its last check-up's EFC. A parameter set with fewer than two uncensored cells is excluded from the registered lever and floor, and its smallest censoring EFC is shown as a lower bound (definition 2). The sensitivity lifetime uses the EOC running estimate cap_aged_est_Ah on the regular-operation rows (cyc_condition 1): its centred running median over eleven consecutive rows, first falling below 90 % of the same first check-up capacity, interpolated linearly in EFC between rows.

D29. Cell temperature. For each regular-operation row (cyc_condition 1, cyc_duration_s above zero) the run temperature is the mean of t_start_degC and t_end_degC, weighted by cyc_duration_s. A cell's T is the weighted mean over its rows from its first check-up up to its 90 % crossing (rows whose EFC, total_q_dischg_sum_Ah / 3.0 Ah, does not exceed L), and over all its rows when it is censored; check-up and other rows are excluded (definition 3). A group's T is the mean of its uncensored cells' T.

D30. The lever. Per protocol, s = -(mean ln L at 40 C - mean ln L at 25 C) / (T_40 - T_25) over the uncensored cells, with the group temperatures of D29 (definition 4). Its standard error is sigma x sqrt(1/n_40 + 1/n_25) / (T_40 - T_25), with sigma^2 the pooled within-set variance of ln L, sum of (n_i - 1) s_i^2 over sum of (n_i - 1), over every parameter set of the 48 with at least two uncensored cells; the interval is plus or minus 1.96 standard errors. Sensitivities: the setpoint difference, 15 K, in place of the logged one; and T of the 25 C groups computed without the rows after 23 April 2023, 00:00 UTC, the Peltier failure. The low-temperature lever is the same from 10 to 25 C. ## Stage 3: the lever in a second laboratory's data, the Sandia cells of Preger et al. 2020

D38 to D49 were written, and `predictions_stage3.md` committed and pushed, before any Sandia lifetime or temperature value was computed. Stage 1 and Stage 2 code and results are not edited; Stage 3 code is in `src/stage3/` and imports the Sandia reader and pipeline of `src/stage2/` (written on 16 October before any Sandia file existed) unchanged.

D38. The data. Battery Archive shared the three zips on 23 October 2026 (`inputs/sandia/MANIFEST.md`, with every file's SHA-256 in `inputs/sandia/files_sha256.csv`). They were copied from the author's computer into this study's workspace and unpacked there, each into its own folder; the raw files are not redistributed. Read before the predictions: the file list, sizes and hashes; the column names (all as the reader expects, none missing); a step pass of every time series without temperature (`src/stage2/steps.py`); and the inventory of `inputs/sandia/inventory.csv`, built by `src/stage2/inventory.py`: per cell its conditions, cycle count, capacity checks with their EFC spacing, the lowest check capacity as a share of the first (for the threshold rule only), the temperature channels with the share of rows that hold a value, the sampling interval, and the durations of the rests next to charge and discharge steps. No temperature value was averaged and no lifetime computed.

D39. The cells. 86 cells: LFP 30, NCA 24, NMC 32, with chemistry, ambient, SOC window, charge and discharge rates and replicate letter read from the file names. Rated capacities (the archive's metadata, as the brief states): LFP 1.1, NCA 3.2, NMC 3.0 Ah. A chemistry's temperature series is its 0 to 100 % cells with a 0.5C charge at 15, 25 and 35 C; the 1C discharge series is registered (four cells at 25 and 35 C, two at 15 C) and the 2C series is secondary and unscored.

D40. Steps and ampere-hours. A sample is a rest when |I| < 0.005C; otherwise a charge or a discharge by the sign of the current (discharge negative, confirmed by the discharge counter rising in every discharge sample checked). The files log every 120 s in most cells (10 s or 60 s in some), and the counters reset at every cycle index; the trapezoidal integral of 120 s samples falls 3.7 to 4.1 % short of the counters (median by chemistry), because a step's first interval is lost, so a step's ampere-hours are the counter difference of `process_cells.counter_ah`.

D41. Capacity checks. A discharge counts toward a check when its mean current lies within 0.05C of 0.5C and its last voltage within 0.02 V of the chemistry's lower cut-off, which the files fix at 2.0 V for LFP and NMC (lowest discharge end voltages 1.99 V) and 2.5 V for NCA (2.499 V). Consecutive such discharges form one check, whose capacity is their mean; for the 0 to 100 % cells cycled at 0.5C discharge every such discharge is its own check. A check whose capacity is below half the cell's first check capacity is an interrupted measurement, not a capacity, and is dropped (the count is reported). A check's EFC is the cell's cumulative discharge before it over the rated capacity.

D42. Lifetime and threshold. The EFC at which the check capacity first falls below the threshold fraction of the cell's first check capacity, by linear interpolation between the bracketing checks, whose spacing is the resolution; a cell that never falls below is censored at its last check. The inventory fixes the thresholds: LFP 95 %, because none of its six 1C temperature-series cells at 15 and 25 C reached 90 % (lowest check fractions 0.909 to 0.933, all below 0.95); NCA 90 % and NMC 90 %, whose temperature-series cells all reached 0.80 or lower. For 0 to 100 % cells the sensitivity lifetime of `lifetimes.sensitivity_lifetime` (the per-cycle capacity at the cycling rate) is reported, unscored.

D43. Cell temperature. The rest-duration rule of the brief could not be applied as meant: the cells cycle without rests (the median rest next to a charge or discharge is one zero-current record, 0 s long; no cell begins with a rest of more than a minute; no cell rests 30 minutes at a stretch), so the last sample of an adjacent rest is the temperature at the moment the current switches. On 24 October 2026 the author therefore chose measure (a), the primary, to be the within-cycle swing: for each cycling unit, a cycling discharge that is not part of a check and the charge step before it, the time-weighted mean skin temperature over the discharge minus that over the charge; a cell's measure (a) is its mean over the units up to its lifetime (EFC at the discharge's start not beyond L), all units for a censored cell. It is offset-free and grows with a cell's own heating wherever the discharge is faster than the 0.5C charge; the groups discharged at 0.5C carry no swing and leave the thermal share. Measure (b), secondary, is the mean over the same units of the skin temperature in their adjacent rest records (the zero-current records at the switches). Reported beside them, unscored: the brief's literal measure (cycling mean minus the last sample of each adjacent rest) and the mean of cell minus environment temperature while cycling. A cell's cycling temperature T_cyc is the duration-weighted mean over its units' charge and discharge; a group's temperature for the lever is the mean T_cyc of its uncensored cells. Step means use only samples that hold a temperature; a unit with a missing step mean is skipped. Every unit up to the lifetime is used; no subset is taken.

D44. The lever. Per chemistry, s = -(mean ln L at 35 C - mean ln L at 25 C) / (T_35 - T_25), with the logged group temperatures of D43, on the registered 1C series at its threshold; its 95 % interval is the percentile interval of 10,000 bootstrap resamples of cells, each carrying its ln L and T_cyc, within each ambient (seed 20261024). The 15 to 25 C secant is the low-temperature lever. Sensitivity: the setpoint difference, 10 K, in place of the logged one. A secant with a censored cell in either group is not determinable. Beside each: both laws over the same secant (between the logged group temperatures), at the chemistry's threshold, a 0.5C charge, the series' discharge rate and the 0 to 100 % window, with `src/stage2/laws.py`; and beside NMC, the KIT 25 to 40 C and 10 to 25 C levers of the protocols 0-100 % 0.33/1.00 C and 1.00/1.00 C, read unchanged from `results/stage2_lever.csv`.

D45. Replicate pairs and the floor. Every pair of cells with the same chemistry, ambient, window and rates; groups of four give six pairs that share cells, and the number of groups is given with every count. The pair difference is |L_a - L_b| / mean(L_a, L_b) at the chemistry's threshold; per chemistry its count, mean, median and 95th percentile (numpy's linear interpolation) over pairs with both lifetimes observed, the pairs whose difference is below their resolution (the larger of the two), and the censored pairs, beside the Geslin floor (2.991 % and 8.718 % at 90 %) and the KIT floor (7.33 % and 20.48 %).

D46. The thermal share. As in `src/stage2/analysis.py`: each pair oriented with the cell of larger measure (a) as the warmer; predicted ln ratio -s dT with the chemistry's registered s and dT the difference in measure (a), the swing standing in for the difference in mean cell temperature; observed ln(L_warmer / L_cooler); a tie when the lifetime difference is below the resolution; a pair with one censored cell enters the sign test only when that cell's last check lies beyond the other's lifetime. (a) The share of untied pairs in which the warmer cell lives shorter, with the binomial sign test, one-sided registered and two-sided beside it; (b) the least-squares slope through the origin of observed on predicted ln ratio, with the percentile interval of 10,000 bootstrap resamples of groups; (c) the root mean square of predicted over observed ln ratios. All pairs, by discharge-rate group (1C or below; 2C and 3C), by chemistry, and one pair per group; measure (b) repeated as the secondary.

D47. Back to the module. |s_NCA| times Stage 1's 0.391279 K, with the bootstrap interval of s, and times the measure-(a) differences of the NCA pairs and of all pairs, against the Geslin floor (3.0 % mean, 8.7 % 95th percentile) and half its mean (1.5 %).

P4 holds if LFP's 25 to 35 C s exceeds both NCA's and NMC's, has the sign of Law 2 over the same secant at LFP's threshold, and lies within a factor of three of it (ratio in [1/3, 3]). P5 holds if every chemistry with at least five pairs entering its floor has a mean in [1.5 %, 10 %]. D50. Corrections and readings fixed on 24 October 2026 after structure checks of the time series, before any lifetime or temperature value was computed; `src/stage3/rows.py`, `src/stage3/prepare.py` and `src/stage3/stage3.py` implement them, `results/stage3_prepare.json` holds every count below, and `tests/stage3_synthetic.py` checks the code on made-up files with these features.

(1) Cycle order. In 20 files (all 18 cells at 35 C, and the two NCA cells at 15 C and 2C) the cycler logs the last record of a cycle up to 0.42 s after the first record of the next (60 s for the single such record in each 15 C cell), so the cycle index steps back by one: 9,776 such records, 1,099 to 1,370 in each LFP cell at 35 C and 143 to 271 in each NCA and NMC cell at 35 C. A stable sort of every file's rows by cycle index returns each to the end of its own cycle. In one file (NMC, 25 C, 20 to 80 %, 0.5/3C, cell a) 4,313 rows of cycles 1211 to 1229, more than one cycle below the running maximum, are interleaved with the cell's own cycles 1253 to 1267 within 8,750 rows; they belong to another record and are dropped. The step pass of D38 is repeated with `steps.py` unchanged on the ordered rows of these 21 cells; the other 65 cells keep their step tables, which their rows reproduce exactly.

(2) Ampere-hours. The counters restart at most cycle-index changes, and in these files the next cycle often begins on the last sample of a discharge, which then ends with the counter at zero; `process_cells.counter_ah` (the last value minus the value before the step, or the last alone after a restart) then returns zero or part of the discharge. It differs from the counter's rise in 25,412 of 279,973 discharges in 18 cells and would count 2.8 to 2.9 % of the discharge ampere-hours of the LFP cells at 35 C, 4.5 to 4.6 % of the NMC cells at 35 C and 1C, 42 to 45 % at 2C, and 58 to 66 % of NCA cells c and d at 35 C and 1C. D40's counter difference is therefore taken per sample: a step's ampere-hours are the sum of the counter's increases over the transitions from the sample before the step to its last sample, a decrease counting as a restart after which the new value counts in full. Of the 275,861 decreases of the discharge counter in the ordered rows all but two fall at a cycle-index change, and those two lie in rests of the two 15 C cells. The sum agrees with `counter_ah` in every discharge of the other 68 cells and lies between 0.995 and 1.48 times the trapezoidal integral of the step's own samples (which omits the interval before its first sample) in every discharge of five samples or more. The inventory's ampere-hour columns (counter against integral, checks, check spacing, EFC total, lowest check fraction) were computed with `counter_ah` on the rows as logged and are superseded for the 23 cells concerned by `results/stage3_prepare.json`; the file is left as committed. D42's thresholds stand as registered.

(3) Temperature pass. The ordered rows are labelled exactly as in `steps.py` and must reproduce the step table in number, kind and start time, or the computation stops. A step's mean temperature is the trapezoidal mean over its consecutive pairs of samples that both hold a value, the mean of its valid samples when it has no such pair (a one-record rest), and missing when none holds a value; its last value is its last valid sample.

(4) A cell's T_cyc pools its units: the sum over their charge and discharge steps of step mean times duration, over the summed duration; cell minus environment likewise.

(5) Units are cycling discharges that are not part of a check (D43), so the 0 to 100 % cells discharged at 0.5C have none. For every cell discharged at 0.5C the measures are reported but not used: its groups leave the thermal share for both measures, P6 and the pair differences of D47; the floor keeps every pair.

(6) A censored cell's units are all of its units, with no EFC limit.

(7) The half-capacity rule of D41 is applied against the first check as measured; the sensitivity lifetime of D42 is `lifetimes.sensitivity_lifetime` unchanged, on the ampere-hours of (2).

(8) Law 1 is not evaluated outside its printed temperatures (Stage 1's code returns no value there); Law 2 at LFP's threshold is Stage 1's coefficients solved for a 5 % loss (`laws.L_law2`).

(9) The KIT floor beside the Sandia floor is read from `results/stage2_lever.json` (mean, median, 95th percentile, pairs).
